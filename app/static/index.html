AI-assisted triage of dermoscopic images

Upload a dermoscopic image of a skin lesion. The model estimates the probability of seven diagnoses from the ISIC 2018 challenge, shows where it looked, and suggests whether the case should be referred to a dermatologist. It supports, and never replaces, clinical judgement.

3 · Example hospital workflow

Illustrative integration at a fictional facility, Himalaya District Hospital, where a dermatologist visits only part of the week. Not a real deployment.

  1. Capture. A health assistant takes a dermoscopic image at the outpatient clinic.
  2. Triage. DermAssist returns class probabilities, a Grad-CAM map and a refer / routine suggestion.
  3. Queue. Referred cases are listed for the visiting dermatologist, highest melanoma probability first.
  4. Review. The dermatologist examines the patient and decides on biopsy or follow-up. The AI output is advisory only.

Limitations

  • Trained and tested on one public dataset (HAM10000 / ISIC 2018), mostly from European clinics and lighter skin types. Performance on patients in Nepal is unknown.
  • Test results come from a held-out split of the same dataset, not from a prospective clinical study.
  • Rare classes (dermatofibroma, vascular lesions) have few test images, so their figures are uncertain.
  • Grad-CAM shows which regions influenced the output; it does not prove the reasoning is clinically correct.
DermAssist is a student research prototype for demonstration. It is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis. Data: ISIC 2018 Task 3 / HAM10000 (Codella et al., 2019; Tschandl et al., 2018).
